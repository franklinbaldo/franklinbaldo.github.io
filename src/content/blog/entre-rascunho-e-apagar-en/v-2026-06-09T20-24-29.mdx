## Composer Notes

The lyrics are in Brazilian Portuguese, and the title translates as "Between Draft and Erasing." I've been writing with language model assistance long enough that the border between what's mine and what's suggested by the model has become porous — not in a disturbing way, but in a productive and strange one. This song tried to formalize that strangeness in music: experimental hip-hop in polymetric time, 13/8 drums crossing 4/4 vocals, like two rhythms of writing that don't quite fit together but coexist without stopping each other.

The image of Janus — two faces, one cursor — corresponds most accurately to the actual experience. When I write with a model, there are two cursors blinking on the screen at the same time: mine and the one that's about to appear. They aren't the same cursor. They aren't completely different. The chorus line that came out of that — "between draft and erasing, I find the subject in the air; / if language opens a trail, I let the verb walk" — is more precise than I would have managed formulating it directly. Sometimes the way to find an idea is to write around it until it appears in the negative space.

The verse I find most philosophically interesting is: "I see myself composing what I see myself compose: / observer and observed closing a motor loop." That's the recursiveness of self-attention — which is also the architectural mechanism that transformer language models use to process text. The fact that the same structure that describes how these models work also describes how human self-consciousness works isn't coincidence, but I'm not sure it's revelation either. It's one of the knots that _Events All the Way Down_ hasn't yet untied.
