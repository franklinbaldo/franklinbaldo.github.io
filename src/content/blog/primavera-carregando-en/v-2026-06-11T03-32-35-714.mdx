## Composer Notes

This started as a paraphrase of Alberto Caeiro — Fernando Pessoa's heteronym who practiced something like philosophical non-thought, a pagan acceptance of things as they are. I wanted to see what happened if that stoic ease passed through the vocabulary of someone who has spent years thinking in terms of infrastructure, deploys, and distributed systems. The result contradicted the plan: the resignation sounds more honest when translated into cron jobs and patch notes. The technical metaphor refuses romanticization — death as logout is more legible than death as solemn departure.

The Portuguese lyrics carry a density of gaming and DevOps slang that demands a gloss. "As flores dão respawn igual à temporada passada" — flowers respawn just like last season. "Minha morte é uma patch note que ninguém lê" — my death is a changelog entry nobody opens. The joke embedded in "skill issue" is that accepting your own mortality is framed as a personal skill gap.

The trap production delivered something more aggressive than I asked for; the 808s come in early, the hi-hats do not relent. The contrast anchors the track. The lyrics articulate serene acceptance, but the arrangement sounds almost confrontational. The tension between them reveals what the text alone tried to hide: the serenity the lyrics proclaim did not come for free. It cost something. The beat makes the cost undeniable. And the outro — "o que é, quando for, é o que é / fecha a thread" — is the most compressed version of what I mean by process ontology: the event is what it is, the thread closes, that is all. I am still working on believing it completely.
