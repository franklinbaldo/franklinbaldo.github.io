There is a corner of my online life that almost nobody sees, and that has quietly become a parallel diary: my [Suno profile](https://suno.com/@franklinbaldo). It currently holds 92 tracks. None of them came from a career plan — they came from stubbornness. I wanted to hear certain things that nobody was going to record for me, so I started recording them with a model.

What began as a toy turned into a private literary jukebox.

## The personas

The part I enjoy most is inventing voices. On Suno I keep a small recurring cast:

- **Irineu**, ceremonial and slow, somewhere between liturgy and a story told on a porch;
- **Caetano**, my excuse to attempt a Brazilian voice drenched in tropicália without offending the original;
- **Maria Bonita**, who shows up when I want leather, backlands, and contained violence;
- and **two Borges** — one in Portuguese, one in English — because I have never decided whether I prefer the translated Borges or the Borges who recites as if he were decoding a cipher.

None of these are anyone's real voice. They are masks. And like every good mask, they say more about who wears them than about who is being imitated.

## The playlists

I organized the songs into collections that, reading them back, feel like chapters of a book I would not know how to write in prose:

- **Meditação** (11 tracks) — slow ambient, to calm the dog and the head;
- **Aleph caipira** (8) — Borges meets the _viola_, the point that contains every point as seen from a porch in Rondônia;
- **Brazil philosophy** (10) — attempts to set to music the ideas I keep chewing on;
- **Borges (PT)** (11) and **Borges (EN)** (3) — because translating Borges into song is a habit I have no intention of dropping.

People click expecting a single and find an essay. Fair enough. That is how I listen, too.

## Why this matters to me

Suno is the kind of tool that forces you to explain what you want to hear _before_ you hear it. That is an odd exercise: in the middle of writing the prompt, you discover you didn't want _bossa_ — you wanted _bossa with short breath and a dirty guitar_. You didn't want _Borges_ — you wanted _Borges as if Riobaldo were reading him aloud_.

I don't know if this is music. I know it is writing.

And I know it is the most intimate place of my online presence: 17 followers, no algorithm pushing anything, no metric to chase. Just the folder growing, and now and then a track that surprises me.

If you are going to listen, start with _Aleph caipira_. That is the one that translates me best right now.
