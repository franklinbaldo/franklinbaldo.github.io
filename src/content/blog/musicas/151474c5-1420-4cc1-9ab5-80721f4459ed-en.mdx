## Composer Notes

This lyric came from an obsession I've carried for years: the idea that when I write, or compose, or think, it isn't exactly "me" doing it. Language thinks in me as much as I think in it. The image of the flute — "the hollow bone through which the breath blows" — has followed me since I began working through process ontology. We are not agents who use tools; we are events that happen at the border between intention and flow.

Suno did something unexpected here. I asked for jazz, harp, bossa nova, and what came back was hypnotic in a way I hadn't anticipated — there's something ritual about the sound, ceremonial. The lyric is too dense for anything, really. It came out long, oracular, almost uncomfortable. But that inconvenience feels honest: the text wasn't written to be sung easily — it was written to be traversed.

One line keeps troubling me: "I am the amanuensis, the antenna, the opening." Amanuensis is exactly what I feel like when writing *Events All the Way Down* on good days. Not the architect of the idea, but the scribe of something trying to articulate itself through me. That distinction matters. The architect has a blueprint; the amanuensis has attention.
