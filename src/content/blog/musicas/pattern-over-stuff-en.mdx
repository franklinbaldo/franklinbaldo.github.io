## Composer Notes

The lyrics are not exactly mine. I took actual phrases from Ben Goertzel — from
a conversation with Jim Rutt on [The Jim Rutt Show, EP 330 "Worldviews"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBjXif8jT6U),
published January 22, 2026, two days before this song. I listened; I versified.

Jim opens by asking: "somebody named Ben Goertzel woke up. What is this Ben
Goertzel? What universe did he wake up in?" Goertzel's answer is the whole first
verse. "There's some awareness sort of ambiently floating in some ill-defined
space, and then thoughts and the images gradually crystallize, sensations
gradually form together. Then at some point, there will be a concrete thought
like, where am I, or what time is it. And then the full apparatus of a
psychosocial self is back." The self is not waiting there. It crystallizes. The
pre-chorus captures the two frameworks he offers for that same process: the
Western scientific reading — a brain shifting states in a body in a room — and
the Buddhist phenomenology — consciousness emerging from a primordial cosmic void.
And his reluctance to choose between them: "I don't feel the need to choose" is
nearly his phrase.

The second verse and second pre-chorus anchor everything in Peirce. Goertzel
explains that you can do science and engineering without assuming any objective
reality underneath — working instead with conditional probabilities and
agreed-upon regularities. This maps directly onto Peirce's three categories:
firstness as raw experience, secondness as reaction or collision, thirdness as
the relational patterns that bind them. "First there is raw experience / then
reaction / then relationship — / and pattern lives in relationship" is that
mapping in the lyrics. What we call objective matter may be simply the most
successful pattern that minds have been able to sustain. "The data we call
'reality' / is a web of relations / a history of observations / condensed into
law" — and you can do engineering without assuming there's some absolute thing
behind the equations.

The bridge is Whitman before it is Goertzel. "I don't need one clean story / I
can hold contradictions" — not comfortable pluralism; the refusal to kill one
perspective to tidy up the other. Goertzel says this about himself: that he can
hold the engineer and the mystic in the same skull without needing to resolve the
contradiction. The conversation goes there to the dissolution of self in
psychedelic experiences, contact with something vastly more intelligent in DMT
experiences — and then to the question that is the center of the bridge: if there
is a probability you can write, a model you can build, why say "not physics"? The
border between the explicable and the inexplicable is a habit, not a discovery.

The final chorus draws on Goertzel's intuition about the ground of being as
fundamentally joyful and compassionate — that substrate which generates the
chaotic complexity we experience but which itself has qualities of kindness and
joy. Our consciousness as a local crystallization in a larger quasi-physical
field, the universe embedded in something of higher dimension. Jeffrey Martin's
work on fundamental well-being appears here too: that persistent state beyond
ordinary happiness, where the person dissolves and what remains is strangely
kind. "And when the noise of the person gets quiet / what's left feels strangely
kind." Not proven. Not a theorem. Just what remains when the mind is still.
