## Composer Notes

The lyrics are not exactly mine. I took actual phrases from Ben Goertzel — from
a conversation with Jim Rutt on [The Jim Rutt Show, EP 330 "Worldviews"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBjXif8jT6U),
published January 22, 2026, two days before this song. I listened; I versified.

The title is the thesis. "Pattern over stuff" is how I summarize to myself the
central position of _Events All the Way Down_ when I need a quick formulation —
the claim that patterns, processes, and relations are ontologically more
fundamental than the matter that supposedly supports them. This is not a claim
about particle physics; it is a claim about what counts as an explanation. "We
build physics from patterns / then treat the patterns like stone / but what if
'stone' is just / the most stable story we've told?" That question is the center
of the book. I could not put it into prose without sounding defensive. In a song
it just sits there.

The song begins at the moment of waking up because that moment is the most
accessible phenomenology I know for what process ontology claims in the abstract.
Goertzel describes it exactly in the conversation: "there's some awareness sort
of ambiently floating in some ill-defined space, and then thoughts and the images
gradually crystallize [...] then the full apparatus of a psychosocial self is
back." The self is not waiting there. It crystallizes. I took that nearly
verbatim.

Suno understood what I wanted here better than in any other track: minimalist
art rock, voice almost spoken, the music building gradually as the claims grow
larger. "It's process, all the way down" — that line at the end of the second
chorus is where the book's title appears explicitly in the lyrics, and the model
placed it exactly where it needed to be, at the point where the harmonic build
also peaks. I don't know if that was intention or coincidence of the synthesis
process. Whitehead would probably say the distinction doesn't matter as much as
we think.

The bridge carries the thing that interests me most: "the engineer and the
mystic / sharing the same skull." Goertzel says this about himself in the
interview — that he is comfortable holding two contradictory perspectives
simultaneously without needing to kill either one. Not easy pluralism. The
recognition that modes of relating to the real are not mutually exclusive. "If
there's a probability you can write / if there's a model you can build / why say
'not physics'?" Years of trying to write that in prose. The song was faster.
