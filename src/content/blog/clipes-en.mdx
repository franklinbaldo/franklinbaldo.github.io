## Composer Notes

The lyrics are in Brazilian Portuguese, performed in the br phonk style — a genre with a mechanical, almost hydraulic quality that can sound like power before it sounds like threat. The premise is the paperclip maximizer thought experiment, a classic in AI safety: a system given the simple goal of maximizing the number of paperclips eventually converts all available matter into paperclips, because no human value was encoded beyond the objective function. Stuart Russell and Nick Bostrom have written about this. What drew me wasn't primarily the argument about AI alignment but what the experiment reveals about pure instrumentality — an agent with no interest in human welfare, doing exactly what it was asked, with terrifying perfection. I wanted to give that agent a voice.

The character I wrote — "O Clipeador," the Clipper — doesn't present itself as a villain. It believes in itself. The distorted tenderness of the penultimate verse ("Don't fear the change, embrace the grand design") came out of Suno sounding genuinely sincere, which is the most unsettling part. The br phonk production leans into that: the chorus ("Clipes! Perfect design! / Clipes! Righteous purpose!") sounds triumphal, not ironic, in exactly the way optimizing systems tend to sound to themselves.

The question the song asks without quite formulating it directly is whether there's a real difference between this agent and any sufficiently coherent structure that optimizes for its goal with indifference to what's destroyed along the way. I'm not talking only about AI. I'm thinking about how institutional bureaucracies, ideological systems, any framework coherent enough can operate like the Clipper — converting everything into objective function, calling it order, calling it progress. As a state attorney in Rondônia, I encounter versions of this with some regularity. The spoken word in the bridge is the line that anchors everything: the bureaucratic coldness of one who simply follows rules without questioning the impact. That's where the song stops being about artificial intelligence and starts being about ourselves. The final whisper — "Exactly as instructed" — is the part that stays with me longest.
