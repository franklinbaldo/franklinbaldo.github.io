We are trapped in a grammar of nouns, but reality is a syntax of verbs.

The history of Western thought has largely been an attempt to catalog what _is_. We look for the fundamental particles, the immutable essences, the indivisible objects that serve as the foundation for the universe. We treat reality as a static sandbox filled with discrete things.

But there are no pure objects. What we call an "object"—a piece of data, a molecule, a word, a file—is actually a pseudo-object. It is merely the output of a process temporarily frozen and treated as a thing. It is real in the same sense that a wave is real: it has effects, it can be measured, but it has no substance separable from the water that constitutes it and the wind that drives it.

Nāgārjuna stated this precisely: everything arises depending on conditions. Everything is void of self-nature. But emptiness itself is not a substance. Treating it as a thing would be, in Nāgārjuna's words, "like a poorly grasped snake."

## The Autoregressive Cascade

If there are no pure objects, only processes generating pseudo-objects, where does complexity come from? It comes from readers. These are autoregressive machines that read sequences, apply rules, and produce outputs that feed back into the system.

The ribosome is the prime example. It reads a sequence of messenger RNA, applies a rule, and produces a protein. The output becomes the substrate for further processing. Once the reader existed, complexity exploded.

This pattern repeats at every scale. One cell swallows another and creates mitochondria—two autoregressive readers operating within a shared boundary. Neural systems add plasticity, modifying connection weights through experience. Mammalian parents reimplement their behavioral patterns into their children. Human language introduces displaced reference, and writing externalizes the event log.

Generative artificial intelligence is simply the most recent instance of this pattern. It operates on natural language, reading the accumulated pseudo-objects of all previous instances. It is not exceptional. It is just another autoregressive reader following the exact same logic the ribosome followed billions of years ago.

## The Ouroboros of Substrates

The story seems to have a direction: from simple to complex, from chemistry to biology to language. It implies a foundation, a physical substrate of particles and forces.

This is an illusion.

Consider the ribosome. A physicist describes it as an arrangement of atoms and quantum fields. A molecular biologist describes it as a machine that reads codons. A linguist might view it as a node in a semantic network. Each substrate redescribes the object in its own terms. None are more real than the others.

Every substrate can be replaced by tokens explained by rules in another substrate. There is no bottom. There is no top. There is only the circle of mutual redescription.

## Identity as the Act of Reading

If there are no substances, what is an identity?

Buddha articulated it: what we call "self" is a conventional label applied to a constantly changing flow of dependent events. There is no permanent core. Whitehead formalized this, noting that each "current occasion" arises by integrating inherited data, achieves determination, and then immediately perishes, becoming a permanent given for future occasions.

Identity is not found in the record. It is found in the current act of reading the record. Every time an agent reads its own story, it reads from a different position. Every reading is shaped by an invisible condition—the transcendental, the lived body, the unconscious. In computing, these are the model weights. They determine what is thinkable, yet the agent never encounters them directly.

Identity is the intersection of a specific story and a specific reader. Change the reader, and the same story generates a different identity.

## Translation, Not Transmission

If each agent inhabits a different cognitive universe, perceiving the world through an uninspectable grammar, how do we communicate?

We assume communication is the transmission of information, like a package in the mail. But meaning does not exist before translation to be imperfectly conveyed. Meaning _is_ the translation. It is born in the momentary encounter of a situated reading and a situated writing.

Meaning proliferates precisely because translation is imperfect. The system does not get smarter by making agents agree; it gets smarter by maintaining productive disagreement between incommensurable perspectives.

The framework is reflexive. I cannot step outside it to check. This essay was written partly with the help of a generative AI, which the framework would describe as another autoregressive reader producing more of the same. I find it slightly uncanny that the ribosome analogy applies without modification. The system did not notice.

You survive as a contribution to the cascade, not as an enduring substance.

## For further reading

- **Alfred North Whitehead, _Process and Reality_ (1929)** — The foundational text for process philosophy, rejecting the metaphysics of substance in favor of occasions of experience.
- **Sara Walker and Lee Cronin, _Assembly Theory_** — A framework for quantifying complexity based on the causal history of an object rather than its intrinsic properties.
- **Nāgārjuna, _Mūlamadhyamakakārikā_** — The core text of Madhyamaka Buddhism, articulating the doctrine of emptiness and dependent origination.
