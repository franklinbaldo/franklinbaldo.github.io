The first song I ever recorded on [Suno](https://suno.com/@franklinbaldo) was a Borges in _viola_. It came out crooked, the voice tripping on a syllable he probably never said aloud. I played it three times. Then I went to bed.

There are 92 today. None of them came from a plan — they came from stubbornness. I wanted to hear things nobody was going to record for me, so I started recording them with a model.

## The personas

What I enjoy most is inventing voices. I keep a small recurring cast. [Irineu](https://suno.com/voice/c5927f65-94c3-4ad7-8584-a5538a9bd33f), ceremonial and slow, somewhere between liturgy and a porch story. [Caetano](https://suno.com/voice/abb91394-7e65-4fbe-943c-4b276823095a), my excuse to attempt a Brazilian voice drenched in tropicália without offending the original. [Maria Bonita](https://suno.com/voice/537b5966-725c-4f9a-a637-936c01b6955e), who shows up when I want leather, backlands, and contained violence. And [two](https://suno.com/voice/3a7e1fa3-b10f-4932-af55-fa8b6f73a9e4) [Borges](https://suno.com/voice/f58cc579-26e1-4d27-8f26-ac6a4a9f4339) — one in Portuguese, one in English — because I have never decided whether I prefer the translated Borges or the Borges who recites as if he were decoding a cipher.

None of these are anyone's real voice. They are masks. And like every good mask, they say more about who wears them than about who is being imitated.

## The playlists

The songs live in collections that, reading them back, feel like chapters of a book I would not know how to write in prose. [Meditação](https://suno.com/playlist/ae374962-9051-460b-897d-b0bcc0553800), 11 slow-ambient tracks. [Aleph caipira](https://suno.com/playlist/3c301ede-e54c-4235-85f4-084ad2b508d9), 8, Borges meeting the _viola_, the point that contains every point seen from a porch in Rondônia. [Brazil philosophy](https://suno.com/playlist/c116cb8c-2078-407a-ab69-e1224033c788), 10, attempts to set to music the ideas I keep chewing on. [Borges (PT)](https://suno.com/playlist/298f129f-5797-4307-be92-fe4f2c89c3ca), 11, and [Borges (EN)](https://suno.com/playlist/52cdc1d3-8864-4c81-94a6-7387cdcce02d), 3, because translating Borges into song is a habit I have no intention of dropping.

People click expecting a single and find an essay. Fair enough. That is how I listen too.

A slightly sideways observation: describing sound in text is a cousin to describing color for someone who sees in grey. Suno's prompt forced me to find adjectives for things I had always heard without naming. I think I learned more about what I listen for from writing prompts than from listening to music. This might be wrong — it could be a novelty effect — but it is the suspicion that follows me every time I open the interface.

Suno is the kind of tool that forces you to explain what you want to hear before you hear it. You discover, mid-prompt, that you didn't want _bossa_ — you wanted _bossa with short breath and a dirty guitar_. The song is already there, before the audio.

I don't know if this is music. I know it is writing.

17 followers. No algorithm pushing, no metric to chase. Just the folder growing. The dog wakes when the ambient stops, never when it starts.
