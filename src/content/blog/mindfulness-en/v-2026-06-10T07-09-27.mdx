Encontre uma posição confortável, seja sentado ou deitado.

Certifique-se de que a coluna esteja ereta, mas sem rigidez, e que os ombros estejam relaxados.

Feche os olhos suavemente e permita-se estar completamente presente aqui e agora.

[Pause]

Sinta o movimento sutil do peito e do abdômen.

[Pause]

Agora, traga sua atenção gentilmente para o corpo.

Comece pelos pés.

Perceba as sensações presentes nos pés, sejam elas de contato, calor ou pressão.

[Pause]

## Composer Notes

The idea came from exhaustion: I needed a guided meditation that wouldn't treat me like a spiritual toddler. Most audio in this genre oscillates between the saccharine and the condescending — a bamboo flute in the background and a voice that sounds like it just drank chamomile tea on a mountaintop. I wanted the opposite: a dry instruction, a sequence of bodily attention that didn't claim to heal my soul, only to observe if my foot was still there. I wrote a text that is almost clinical in structure — feet, legs, abdomen, chest — because, apparently, I will take orders from a doctor, but I refuse them from a guru.

Then comes the ready-made joke technology played on me. I went to Suno to ask for this clinical track and proposed the tags "ambient, new age, classical, calm." The machine took my orthopedic instruction manual and delivered an arrangement that supports silence without trying to fill it. The marked pauses in the text became actual spaces in the recording. The generative model, trained on the exact new-age aesthetic I wanted to avoid, filtered my coldness and returned it as calm. I wanted to escape the cliché and outsourced the task to a statistical engine trained on clichés.

There's a theoretical irony I also don't hide: I write about process ontology, about the world as an unbroken fabric of events, and at the end of the day, I need a four-minute track generated by artificial intelligence to remember to breathe. The convergence between Whitehead and mindfulness exists — both agree that the world is not made of things, it is made of passages. The difference is that I write philosophy with my eyes open and I listen to meditation with my eyes closed. This track doesn't resolve the ontological problem; it only tries to make me forget about it for a moment.
