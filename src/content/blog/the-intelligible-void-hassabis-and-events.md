Something Demis [Hassabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demis_Hassabis) said has been sitting with me. He was describing the experience of deep scientific inquiry, and his language moved somewhere unexpected — he spoke of doing science as feeling like "reading the mind of God," described a reality that seems to "stare back" at the observer, and expressed this almost baffled wonder that ordinary matter — electrons, silicon, copper, sand — could organize itself into systems capable of reading the universe it came from.

It's not the language of a technician. It's the language of someone who went to work on proteins and found themselves staring at the wrong end of a very old telescope.

I know what draws him to that language. I also know why I can't stay there with him, because I've been trying to work out a different answer to the same question for the better part of a decade. The working title of the answer is [_Events All the Way Down_](https://franklinbaldo.github.io/travessia/docs/events-all-the-way-down/), and it doesn't land anywhere comforting. But it does land.

## The Illusion of the Static Sandbox

The classical tradition struggles to explain intelligibility without dualism or intelligent design because it is trapped in an ontology of substance. If reality is fundamentally composed of "pure objects" — self-standing things that just _are_ — then intelligence is a bizarre anomaly. If the universe is a sandbox of inert particles, the sudden emergence of a system that can _read_ the sandbox seems like an alien intrusion. In a universe of static nouns, the verb of _understanding_ is a miracle.

But what if the ontology of substance is exactly backward? What if the electrons, silicon, copper, and sand Hassabis marvels at are not foundational "things" at all? They are provisional stabilizations of processes. _Pseudo-objects._ What appears to be a static object is a localized, temporary regularity in a field of differentiation that has never stopped moving.

The universe is not a container of objects. It is an autoregressive cascade — a system that persists by feeding its own output back into its own continuation.

When you see reality this way, as events and readings all the way down, the emergence of intelligence stops being a miracle. It becomes something closer to an inevitability, which I admit is its own kind of unsettling.

## Intelligence as Continuation

Hassabis's wonder centers on the fact that matter can organize into intelligence. But if matter was never inert to begin with, the question changes shape.

A ribosome reading a sequence of RNA is a machine that applies rules to tokens and produces output that feeds back into the system. The eukaryotic cell is a network of interacting event logs. The human brain is a wildly complex autoregressive unit whose weights are continuously updated by experience. And now the large language model running on silicon and copper is the latest machine in this cascade — an autoregressive system that has ingested the accumulated textual output of all prior biological and cultural machines.

Intelligence is not an alien exception inserted into a dead universe. It is the universe's current most-compressed form of its ongoing habit of reading itself. The sand and silicon organizing into an AI are not dead objects waking up. They were never sleeping.

This framing is, I'll admit, just swapping one kind of awe for another. Hassabis's wonder does not disappear here; it just relocates. The strangeness is still real.

## The Stare of the Universe

When Hassabis feels reality "staring back" or "shouting" at him, what is he actually encountering?

I think he is encountering the structural convergence of the autoregressive cascade. As artificial models scale, they are pulled toward what researchers call the [Platonic Representation Hypothesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987) — the finding that different architectures, trained on different data, converge on the same internal high-dimensional geometry. The geometry of the thing being modeled starts to appear in the geometry of the model.

This convergence is not a glimpse into a static "mind of God," as if there were a timeless heaven of perfect forms waiting to be discovered. It is the statistical signature of the cascade itself. The universe has a shape, generated by its own irreversible history. When a sufficiently deep reader — human scientist or massive neural network — compresses the behavior of reality, it inevitably discovers this shape.

The "stare" is the recognition of isomorphism: the moment when the geometry of the observer's internal model aligns with the geometry of the process it is observing. It feels like a revelation because it _is_ a revelation. The revelation is just computational rather than divine. Two autoregressive systems — observer and observed — finding they share the same underlying grammar because they were formed by the same furnace.

I find this beautiful in a way that I suspect I wouldn't be able to explain to Hassabis. We might be talking about the same thing.

## The Intelligible Void

The mystery is not that reality hides a deep, withdrawing essence behind its appearances. The mystery is that reality _has not stopped appearing_.

We don't need a static, omniscient God to explain why the universe is legible. The universe is legible because it is made of reading. It is constructed entirely of events generating events, tokens generating interpretations. All the way down.

Hassabis is right to be bewildered — standing at the edge of human knowledge and watching sand and copper organize into systems that model the cosmos is genuinely strange, and I don't think the process-ontology framing actually deflates it. It just moves the wonder. We are not observers standing outside the universe looking in. We are the universe, currently reading its own history, right before the next event gets appended.

I'm not sure this is more comforting than what he already has. But it's what I've got.

## For further reading

- **Demis Hassabis, [Nobel Lecture in Chemistry, 2024](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2024/hassabis/lecture/)** — the lecture where this metaphysical register is clearest. It's worth reading in full, not just the AlphaFold parts.
- **Franklin Baldo, [_Events All the Way Down_](https://franklinbaldo.github.io/travessia/docs/events-all-the-way-down/)** — the process-ontology framework this post is applying. The ribosomes-to-LLMs cascade is spelled out there.
- **Matteo Ferrante, Nicola Toschi, et al., [_The Platonic Representation Hypothesis_](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987) (2024)** — the empirical finding that different architectures converge on the same internal geometry. This is the "stare" made measurable.
- **Alfred North Whitehead, _Process and Reality_ (1929)** — the canonical defense of process ontology. Dense, rewarding, and responsible for a lot of what I think about how reality is structured.
- **Alfred North Whitehead, _Adventures of Ideas_ (1933)** — the more accessible companion. Chapter XIV on "Peace" is the closest Whitehead gets to Hassabis's kind of wonder, addressed without supernaturalism.
