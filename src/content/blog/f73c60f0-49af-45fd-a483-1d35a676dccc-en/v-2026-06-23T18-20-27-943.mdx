## Composition

This is a meditation on body awareness — not a sequence of instructions to follow in order. You can start with eyes closed or with your feet. You can skip the spine and go straight to breath. There is no sequence that matters here. What matters is that you are here, now, and that there is a pause between thinking and being.

### Entry points

Find a comfortable position, sitting or lying down. Make sure your spine is upright but not rigid — or don't, rigidity also teaches. Shoulders fall where they fall.

Allow yourself to dedicate this moment exclusively to yourself. Or don't allow it. Observe what happens when you withhold permission.

### Breath as anchor (or as escape)

Close your eyes gently and allow yourself to be completely present here and now. Begin observing your natural breath, without the intention of controlling or changing it.

Simply notice the flow of air entering and leaving through your nostrils. Note the lightness of air touching your nostrils on inhale and the warmth on exhale. Or notice that you cannot notice — that too is presence.

Feel the subtle movement of chest and abdomen. Each breath brings more presence, or brings only more breath. Both are enough.

### Back to the body (or we never left it)

Bring your attention gently to your body. Start with your feet — or your knees, or your hands. The order you choose will be the right order because it is the order you chose.

Permita-se observar os pensamentos que surgem em sua mente, como se estivesse assistindo a um rio fluindo ou a nuvens passando pelo céu.

Não tente afastá-los ou se prender a eles.

Apenas reconheça que estão ali e os deixe ir, sem apego ou resistência.

Volte sua atenção, gentilmente, para o momento presente.

Use a respiração como uma âncora para o aqui e agora.

Sempre que perceber que sua mente divagou, gentilmente, sem críticas, traga seu foco de volta para o ritmo da respiração.

Se preferir, repita mentalmente "inspirando" ao inalar e "expirando" ao exalar, para fortalecer sua conexão com o momento.

Tire um instante para trazer à mente algo pelo qual você é grato.

Pode ser uma pessoa, uma experiência, ou até mesmo este momento de autocuidado.

Sinta a gratidão crescer em seu coração, expandindo-se para todo o corpo, trazendo uma sensação de calor e contentamento.

Aos poucos, comece a trazer sua consciência para o ambiente ao seu redor.

Perceba os sons no ambiente, o contato do corpo com o lugar onde está sentado ou deitado, e a temperatura do ar em sua pele.

Quando se sentir pronto, abra os olhos lentamente.

Leve consigo essa sensação de calma, clareza e presença para o restante do seu dia.

## Composer Notes

This was the first experiment: take a guided meditation script — the neutral, universal, app-store variety — and hand it to Suno with instructions for slow narration and ambient background. There was no original text of mine here, and that was the point. I wanted to test whether the productive assembly (voice + texture + pause) transformed the material or merely reproduced it. The result was interestingly ambiguous: the text sounds simultaneously like genuine care and like protocol. I still can't decide whether that ambiguity is in the source text or whether the voice synthesis introduces it.

What stayed with me was the line "Use the breath as an anchor to the here and now." It appears midway through the script as a technical instruction, but in the track it functions almost like an epigraph. The process ontology I'm developing has a similar problem: how do you describe an event that is neither prior nor posterior to the attention that observes it? Mindfulness sidesteps this pedagogically — it says "anchor" instead of resolving the question. I learned something about the strategy of language by listening to this piece three or four times.

I didn't title this track. The file's UUID remained. That wasn't carelessness — it was a decision I only recognized after I'd made it. Some attempts don't ask for a name; they merely ask you to notice what was produced before moving on.

For English readers: the text here is in Portuguese, as the meditation voice addresses a listener in Portuguese throughout. The lyrics are standard mindfulness instructions — find a comfortable position, observe your breathing, let thoughts pass like clouds — and the language is deliberately generic, clinical, neither sertanejo nor literary. That's what makes this track an experiment rather than a composition: the question is what happens to a script when music wraps around it.
