# Electromagnetic Architecture Theorem

D/B, medium confidence. The interesting change in version 2 is methodological rather than evidential: it now distinguishes mathematical repackaging from independent physical tests.

## r293 — version 2

The framework links a set of central-motion quantities such as rv^2, r^2g and related areal/volumetric rates, and interprets their cross-domain organization electromagnetically. Version 2 explicitly repairs dimensional presentation and separates four categories that had previously been blurred together: algebraic identities, quantities defined by the model, empirical regularities and independent electromagnetic tests.

That distinction matters. Equalities such as rv^2 = r^2g under circular-orbit substitutions can be useful organizing relations, but they do not by themselves show that electromagnetism causes gravity or orbital structure. The physical claim requires measurements that are not already encoded in the same r, v and g variables.

The revised manuscript points to magnetic and electric fields, plasma density, current density, Poynting flux, ionization and atmospheric measurements as decisive external tests. That is a productive move toward falsifiability. However, the current record still does not derive the proposed architecture from Maxwell/plasma dynamics or show independent electromagnetic measurements that establish the claimed causal bridge.

An open PREreview of the previous version found that many central relations were algebraic rearrangements, the electromagnetic mechanism was not derived, the empirical comparison lacked independent EM observables and error treatment, and major revision was needed. Version 2 addresses the dimensional/presentation part of that criticism but has not yet closed the deeper dynamical and evidential gaps.

The programme therefore enters as D scientific / B interest rather than F: it is early and under-validated, but the narrowed version is testable and is not presently defined by a central claim already decisively contradicted by established measurements.
