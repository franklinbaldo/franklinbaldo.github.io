# Grid Universe

C/S, medium confidence. The newest work improves robustness and source hygiene more than it improves the case for the microscopic substrate.

## r287 — everpresent-Lambda stress test, holdout prediction and lab discriminator

The latest package adds three useful checks. First, Iteration 121 finds that only 0.007% of 200,000 histories in the project’s simplified everpresent-Lambda implementation satisfy its simultaneous late-time, stability and recombination criteria. This is evidence against that implementation, not a general observational exclusion of causal-set everpresent Lambda because the test fixes the cosmological background and uses project-chosen acceptance criteria.

Second, 200 random half/half splits of 164 SPARC galaxies give a typical holdout error of about 0.145 dex for a measured-gap relation learned only from the training half, compared with about 0.429 dex for visible-only Newtonian gravity. That demonstrates predictive structure inside SPARC, while remaining short of an external validation or a microscopic derivation.

Third, the programme preregisters a torsion-balance discriminator using milligram-scale source masses: a total-field hypothesis predicts the Newtonian far/near ratio, while a source-self-field hypothesis predicts ratios roughly 1.9–3.3 times larger. This is a valuable falsifiable target, but the experiment has not yet been performed and extrapolation from galactic accelerations to the laboratory remains a substantive assumption.

## r278 — viewing-angle robustness

Iteration 120 preregistered a direct test of whether disk deprojection could manufacture the galaxy acceleration pattern. Splitting 2,765 SPARC points from 155 galaxies into 30-50, 50-70 and 70-90 degree inclination bins gives residual offsets of -0.003, +0.005 and -0.000 dex, with scatter 0.138, 0.146 and 0.137 dex. The expected extra low-inclination scatter was not seen. This is a useful robustness result: a simple viewing-angle correction artefact does not explain the galaxy relation. It does not, however, identify the microscopic grid or solve the programme's cross-scale cluster mismatch.

## r277 — convention-light defaults and stuck-item sweep

The programme now leads with gas-dominated galaxy points, boundary-free cluster points and explicit mass-to-light variation. Its cluster shortfall survives these choices, while the low-redshift dark-energy feature is modest and dataset-sensitive. The colliding-cluster population check is relevant evidence that unseen gravitating mass tends to track collisionless galaxies rather than hot gas, but the exact Harvey et al. offset statistic used by the ledger has a published methodological challenge and is not treated here as a settled precision bound.

## r276 — cross-scale gravity stress test

Iterations 118-119 show that the galaxy visible-pull relation does not simply extrapolate to groups and clusters. The conventions audit is a useful follow-up because it starts varying borrowed modelling choices rather than treating them as fixed facts.

## r303 — redshift evolution of the acceleration threshold (8 October 2026 UTC)

Iteration 132 preregistered a discriminant between a constant characteristic acceleration and an expansion-tracking threshold, \(a_t(z)=a_0 H(z)/H_0\). Its own fit to six Genzel et al. galaxies is inconclusive (reported chi-square about 0.2 versus 1.1), with substantial uncertainties and model choices for enclosed baryons. This is **not a positive detection** of a discrete-space mechanism.

Ciocan et al., *MUSE-DARK III* (2026, arXiv:2604.22613), independently of the Grid project, found an increasing radial-acceleration-relation (RAR) characteristic scale in 79 galaxies at \(0.33<z<1.44\), with \(a_0(z\approx1)\) near \(2.38\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\). Their inference uses a model-based 3D decomposition of stellar, gaseous and dark components, including pressure corrections, and is sensitive to mass/gas systematics. Grid's \(H(z)\) proposal agrees under an adopted SPARC local anchor but rises too slowly under the survey's own extrapolated zero-redshift intercept; the preregistered expectation was therefore only partly met. A reanalysis by Alcock (MNRAS 2026; arXiv:2609.38796) finds a floating-amplitude \(H(z)\) scaling statistically compatible with the same MUSE-DARK bins. **That is an independent analysis of the same observational sample, not an independent replication.** The \(a_0\)–Hubble coincidence also predates Grid.

**For:** a preregistered phenomenological confrontation, transparent accounting for normalization dependence and contrary Tully–Fisher indications. **Against / unresolved:** no observation unique to Grid, no microscopic derivation of the scaling, outstanding cross-scale cluster discrepancy and uncertain gas conventions. **Discriminator:** a genuinely held-out galaxy sample and a frozen local normalization/stellar+gas prescription; failure of the shape or of the cluster extrapolation would weaken Grid.

**Clash with Asymptotic Safety (A/A), on observational discrimination versus UV completion:** Grid poses a near-term galaxy-redshift test but lacks a demonstrated UV quantum theory; Asymptotic Safety has a mature renormalization programme but no settled unique galaxy-acceleration prediction. Neither gains a tier from this dataset. **Scientific C / interest S, medium confidence: unchanged.**
