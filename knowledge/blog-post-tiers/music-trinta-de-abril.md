summary: >-
  A strong and memorable transposition of Borges's annual visits for Beatriz Viterbo into moda de viola. The work makes
  repetition do thematic work: April thirtieth, the dark suit, Garay Street, cousin Carlos and the returning strophic form
  turn memory into a ritual with a visible cost. Hrönir evidence is mature and unusually well aligned across ordinal,
  absolute and de-confounded signals. Quality remains B because some connective lines are metrically functional rather
  than individually strong, the devotion framing does not fully resolve its own obsession/compulsion ambiguity, and the
  adaptation remains heavily indebted to Borges's narrative furniture. Interest is A because the Brazilian genre
  transplantation and the idea of saudade as a deliberately renewed practice remain distinctive, memorable and generative.
