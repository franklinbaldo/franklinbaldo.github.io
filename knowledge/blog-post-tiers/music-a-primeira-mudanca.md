summary: >-
  A strong transposition of the opening grief-image from Borges's "O Aleph" into moda de viola: the changed
  billboard becomes the first visible unit in an indefinitely continuing process of forgetting, while the adaptation's
  own line "se mudaram o cartaz, vão mudar o meu viver" changes the stance from Borgesian resistance to anticipated
  acceptance. Hrönir evidence is mature and repeatedly rewards the work for memorability, embodied reframing,
  epistemic restraint and practical recategorization of grief. Quality remains B because the central conceit is stronger
  than some of the lyric's line-level execution: several verses rely on generic emotional vocabulary, and the composer
  notes sometimes explain work that the image and song had already accomplished. Interest is A because the genre
  transplantation and philosophical disagreement with Borges remain distinctive, portable and conversation-producing.
