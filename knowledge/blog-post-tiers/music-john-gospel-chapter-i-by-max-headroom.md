summary: >-
  "John Gospel chapter I by Max Headroom" is a sharply conceived cultural-translation experiment: the Johannine Prologue is recast as an unstable 1980s broadcast, with Max Headroom's stutter and signal glitches turning mediation into form rather than merely topic. Interest is A because the collision is immediately legible, memorable, and generative; several Hrönir perspectives independently identify the stutter/glitch mechanism as the work's real invention. Quality is B because the execution is uneven around that strong core. Some broadcast jokes are decorative rather than load-bearing, the piece often paraphrases rather than transforms John 1, and the composer notes occasionally state the historical/theological genealogy and the imperfect-transmission analogy more strongly than the text earns. The work is good and distinctive, but its best formal idea is stronger than every line and stronger than some of its explanatory claims.
