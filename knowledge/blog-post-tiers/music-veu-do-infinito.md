summary: >-
  "Véu do Infinito" is most successful as a deliberately preserved failure artifact: a Suno lyric asked to approach the Aleph by computational totalization collapses into cosmic vocabulary, and the composer notes turn that collapse into the work's central observation about scale, restriction, and the need for a veil. Quality is C because the achieved reading experience remains genuinely mixed even after that frame is understood: the lyric is exhausting by design, several of its strongest ideas arrive retrospectively in the notes rather than through the song's own structure, and the Borges/model claims sometimes move from a useful metaphor to a broader diagnosis too quickly. Interest is A because preserving the machine's overproduction rather than cleaning it up creates a distinctive, generative object whose failure can itself be inspected, argued with, and remembered.
