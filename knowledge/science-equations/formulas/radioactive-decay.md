# Radioactive decay law

\[
-\frac{dN}{dt}=\lambda N
\]

Here \(N\) is the number of undecayed nuclei and \(\lambda>0\) is the decay constant. The law is used to model populations of unstable nuclei when each nucleus has a time-independent decay probability.

## Used in

- [Nuclear physics](../branches/nuclear-physics.md)

## Mathematical family

Let \(x=N\) and \(k=\lambda\). Then

\[
\frac{dx}{dt}=-kx,
\]

so this occurrence belongs to [first-order linear decay](../families/first-order-linear-decay.md).

## Evidence

OpenStax lists both the differential radioactive decay rate and its exponential solution \(N=N_0e^{-\lambda t}\) in its nuclear-physics treatment: [University Physics Volume 3, §10.3](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/10-3-radioactive-decay).
