# First-order chemical rate law

\[
\frac{d[A]}{dt}=-k[A]
\]

For a simple first-order reaction, \([A]\) is the reactant concentration and \(k>0\) is the first-order rate constant. Integrating gives \([A]_t=[A]_0e^{-kt}\).

## Used in

- [Chemical kinetics](../branches/chemical-kinetics.md)

## Mathematical family

With \(x=[A]\), the domain notation becomes

\[
\frac{dx}{dt}=-kx.
\]

It is therefore an occurrence of [first-order linear decay](../families/first-order-linear-decay.md).
