reviewA: >-
  In music-o-medo-do-louco, the comedy arises from the imminent and physical
  ridiculousness. The author subverts the transcendent mysticism of Borges'
  short story 'The Aleph', lowering the literary epiphany to the rawness of
  mundane fear: a man being poisoned by a madman in a cellar in the dark. The
  phrase 'praying for it to end soon... That Rite of Spring' and the verse 'he
  put a sack under the nape of my neck' reveal the pathetic nature of Borges'
  situation as narrator. The irony and the macabre, humiliating humor of the
  trapped and frightened character act as argumentative support: if you take
  away the physical fear of death at the hands of Carlos, the 'miracle' of the
  Aleph loses all its epistemological shock value and becomes just ethereal
  poetry. It is the ridiculous humor of the situation (the sack under the neck,
  the distress of the kidnapping) that makes the shock work.
reviewB: >-
  In music-a-primeira-mudanca, there is no trace of humor, much less structural
  humor. The author translates Borges' mourning for Beatriz Viterbo into a
  Pantanal cordel, coating everything with a solemn and melancholic boredom ('My
  God, how much sadness I am perceiving'). From the point of view of our reading
  perspective, the author totally refuses exposure to ridicule or irony to deal
  with something heavy. He protects himself under the gravity of the theme of
  mourning and the 'withdrawal of the universe'. No one will laugh at him,
  because the lament is safe, tearful and conventionally dramatic. If there is
  irony (a cigarette billboard marking the passage of time and death), it does
  not act as a loose laugh that prepares the reader for the punch. The author
  merely laments reverently.
