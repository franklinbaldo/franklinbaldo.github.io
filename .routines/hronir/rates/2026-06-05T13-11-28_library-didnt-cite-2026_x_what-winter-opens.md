review_a: >-
  The Weird-Clarity Reader finds three candidates in Post A. First: 'The
  Goodreads public shelf was always there.' This works. Two sentences after the
  locked archive, a blank statement of what was available the whole time. Try to
  paraphrase it: 'The Goodreads profile was accessible.' That is not the same
  thing. The word 'always' changes the ontology — the shelf was not waiting, it
  was always already there, which is different. Second: 'It is the unwritten
  post the library has been holding since it was read.' This is strange in a
  productive way — a book can hold a post, and a post can remain unwritten, and
  together these ideas make a thing that has no word. Third: 'The library is not
  a manifesto. It is an accumulated result.' Two sentences doing a clean
  inversion. However, the Weird-Clarity Reader also finds forced profundity:
  'informing the grammar' at the end of the Hesse/Kerouac section is an
  explanation that claims strangeness without earning it. And the final section,
  'What the Shelf Reads Back,' recapitulates the essay's claims in a way that
  domesticates the strange things the middle sections built. The chill is real
  in parts; the final section closes the window.
review_b: >-
  Post B has the best single sentence in this comparison, and the Weird-Clarity
  Reader quotes it immediately: 'A sutra my mother recited before I had words to
  refuse it.' Parse this and notice what happens. It is grammatically simple.
  But the temporal structure — something received before the capacity to refuse
  it existed — is doing something that resists paraphrase. You could say 'I was
  taught this religion as a child before I could think critically about it,' and
  that would be flatter, correct, and not the same. The strangeness is in the
  tense and in the word 'words': not the capacity to refuse, but specifically
  the words. The Weird-Clarity Reader also marks: 'I don't have that floor — for
  me it's process all the way down, no rock underneath.' The concrete image for
  an ontological position lands cleanly. And: 'The habit of monitoring came back
  before the habit of training.' This one resists paraphrase too — try to say
  the same thing differently and you lose the gap between monitoring and
  training that the sentence makes visible. Post B is not trying to be weird.
  That is why it gets there.
