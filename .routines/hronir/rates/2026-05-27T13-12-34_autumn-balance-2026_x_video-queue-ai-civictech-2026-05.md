review_a: >-
  Post A's softest claim is 'Generative AI suffers from horror vacui.' Horror
  vacui as a term comes from visual art — the discomfort with unfilled space in
  a composition. Applied to language models, it functions as an atmospheric
  metaphor for the tendency to fill in narrative gaps with generated content
  rather than leave them empty. The metaphor has intuitive appeal, but 'suffers'
  implies something like a structural drive toward gap-filling that is
  observable and measurable, which has not been established here. The strongest
  objector would note that models trained on next-token prediction are doing
  something more specific than 'fearing emptiness' — they're optimizing for
  likely continuations, which is not identical to the literary-psychological
  claim. The author doesn't know this, which means the difficulty was skirted.
  The second claim worth pressing is 'one of the greatest semantic containment
  challenges I've faced' — without a list of the other challenges for
  comparison, this is not a calibrated statement. The good news: the

  prefer not to investigate' is one of the more honest disclaimers in the blog.
