review_a: >-
  music-xadrez adapts Borges's 'Ajedrez' with remarkable fidelity to the
  original's structure (players descending to pieces to the frame-breaking
  question). The composer grounds it in lived experience—as a state attorney
  arguing for systems he didn't design, the feeling of being a piece that thinks
  it's a player becomes professional, not merely philosophical. The connection
  to Wolfram's computational irreducibility is legitimate: a chess game cannot
  be shortcut; neither can the god who knows what the player will do. But the
  execution requires the listener to *already care* about these ideas, or to
  have read the composer notes to understand why they matter. The cinematic
  trip-hop—dusty drums, detuned piano, sub-bass—creates the right mood of
  slow-motion nightmare, but it's atmospheric rather than revelatory. A Curious
  Outsider listening without context hears a moody song about a board game; they
  miss the recursion unless it's explained. The composition is precise. It just
  asks you to bring your own philosophical luggage to the room.
