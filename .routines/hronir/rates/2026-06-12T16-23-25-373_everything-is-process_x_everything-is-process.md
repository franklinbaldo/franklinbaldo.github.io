  identity?

  The answer is old. Buddha articulated it with unsurpassed precision: what we
  call "self" is a conventional label applied to a constantly changing flow of
  dependent events. There is no permanent, unchanging core behind the flow. Take
  away the events and there is nothing underneath.

  Whitehead formalized this: each "current occasion" arises by integrating data
  inherited from previous occasions. It achieves determination, produces its
  output and immediately perishes as an active subject. But it achieves what
  Whitehead calls "objective immortality"—it becomes a permanent given,
  available to be inherited by all future occasions.

  Ricoeur distinguished _idem_ — sameness, objective continuity — from _ipse_ —
  ipseity, the active and interpretative engagement of the self with its own
  history. Identity is not on record. It is in the _current act of reading the
  record_.

  This is Gadamer's hermeneutic circle: we understand the parts in light of the
  whole and the whole in light of the parts. Each time the agent reads his own
  story, he reads it from a different position, because the act of reading has
  become part of the story.

  And every reading is shaped by an invisible condition—what Kant called the
  transcendental, what MerleauPonty called the lived body, what Freud called the
  unconscious. In computing, these are the model weights. They determine

  directly. They reveal themselves only symptomatically — as

  hammer that only becomes visible when it _breaks_.
