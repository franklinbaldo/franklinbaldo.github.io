review_a: >-
  The Delphi essay earns real credit from the skeptical specialist for explicit
  self-acknowledgment: 'I am overstating, and I know it' before the
  Socrates-as-first-installation claim, and the careful hedge on the gnōthi
  seautón etymology ('no Greek philologist would let me get away with stating it
  flatly'). The Perseus links and Plutarch citations survive replacement — you
  cannot swap in a generic ancient-wisdom reference and get the same result. The
  Croesus/Halys example is precise and well-deployed. But the post's softest
  claim is the organizing analogy itself: 'Delphi was a harness.' The skeptical
  specialist's objection is not that the analogy is wrong but that the post
  never tests it adversarially. A harness constrains a powerful system to make
  it usable. The Pythia was not constrained Apollo — she was a person in trance
  whom priests interpreted. The analogy runs from the right conclusion (ritual
  frames make intelligence usable) to an imprecise historical vehicle. The post
  also claims 'the first reader to make know thyself mean introspect clearly
  was, more or less, Descartes, in 1641.' Augustine, a thousand years earlier,
  built the whole interior self as the theater of God's action. The post's 'more
  or less' is doing more work than the author admits. The meme figures are an
  oddity in a piece doing serious historical work — not fatal, but they rupture
  the tonal consistency the essay otherwise maintains.
review_b: >-
  The Conceptual Document post does what the skeptical specialist most rewards:
  it applies the same adversarial reading to itself that the specialist would
  apply from outside. 'Drafting is not the bottleneck. Judgment is.' That claim
  survives hostile substitution — you could replace this spec with any other
  automated-writing pipeline spec and the same gap would appear. The
  acknowledgment that 'doxxing-by-inference' is a real risk but that an LLM
  agent might not reliably catch it is exactly the kind of scoped concession the

  Boswell analogy is the post's weakest point. Boswell was a human with social
  access, physical presence, and the kind of contextual perception that makes
  stumbles legible. The analogy imports Boswell as a label for the aspiration
  without examining what made Boswell effective that an automated system cannot
  replicate. The post is aware that its spec failed to mention the gap between
  events and their weight; this awareness arrives too late and at the end, as a
  footnote rather than a foundation. But the closing admission — 'I have not
  solved it' — is the kind of honest non-closure the skeptical specialist
  prefers to a smooth resolution. The post knows its own roughness and doesn't
  sand it down.
