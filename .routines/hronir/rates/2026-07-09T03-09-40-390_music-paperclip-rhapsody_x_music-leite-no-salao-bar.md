  technological opera with soprano vocals — a choice that immediately raises
  questions for a skeptical reader. The softness: the claim that the paperclip
  maximizer is 'structurally a comedy.' Is that Bostrom's framing, or is the
  composer imposing it? The thought experiment is usually presented as deadly
  serious existential risk. The composer reframes it as comedic through formal
  choice, then credits Suno with 'understanding the joke' in the whispered
  ending. But Suno doesn't understand jokes — it understands patterns in
  training data about how whispered endings function. The Whitehead invocation
  ('a process without values is ontologically empty') is precise and true, but
  it's doing its own work separate from the opera. The equation 'paperclip
  maximizer equals ontological emptiness' sits undefended. Would Bostrom accept
  this reading, or is the composer extrapolating? The most exposed claim is that
  opera was 'the right form' — this reads as aesthetic conviction masquerading
  as structural necessity. The form does work (grandioseness revealing
  absurdity), but the claim that it was 'the right' form needs more than
  resonance to hold.
